## Abstract

The core claim of this report is:

> **The world came from nothing.**

How it came is answered by one sentence:

> **Consciousness assumes there is someone being conscious.**

"Assuming" is the act by which nothing becomes something: an appearance is pointed out, taken by default to exist, and a "must be" about it grows. This act does not happen once. The self, objects, space, time, public knowledge, laws, and finally the world as a whole are each the same act happening again.

Collision operates alongside assuming. Assuming accounts for *that there is* something; collision accounts for *which* something. What has been set up meets results it does not decide for itself, and the world's specific shape is constrained by that.

The report distinguishes two layers of speech. At the **root layer**, nothing and appearance are one; there is no before or after, and nothing can be derived. At the **perspective layer**, the same thing unfolds as an ordered route: nothing, happening, trace, assuming, reidentification, loop, collision, space, time, the public, law, world. Both layers are true. The last section shows that this report is itself that "assuming."

Earlier documents were research drafts for this claim. This report gathers them into one complete origin route and takes precedence over them.

## I. The Core Claim

### 1. The claim

> **The world came from nothing.**

Three words need to be made clear first.

- **Nothing**: not "there is nothing at all," but the place where being and non-being have not yet separated. See Section III.
- **Something**: anything that can be pointed out as "a thing," including "I," a stone, time, a law, and "the world" itself.
- **Came**: not physical production, but being pointed out, attributed, and taken by default to exist. See Section V.

### 2. The mechanism

> **Consciousness assumes there is someone being conscious. This is where everything comes from.**

There is not first an entity called consciousness that then manufactures everything. As consciousness operates, it produces the attribution "there is an I here"; in the same moment it produces the attribution "there is a thing there." That "I" and that "thing" are both products of this one moment.

"Assuming" is not pejorative. It is the way appearance becomes "something."

### 3. What the claim covers

It covers everything that is "something": whatever can be pointed out as a thing comes from here. It explains not only the sense of self but where objecthood, existence, space-time, and laws come from as "things."

## II. Two Layers: Two Ways of Saying One Thing

### 1. The root layer

> "Appearance is the void. Being is also the void."
> — Zhou Peng, *World Root — A Pointing*

At the root layer, the void, appearance, being, existence, and this moment are one. There is no before or after, no producer, only producing.

The root layer cannot be derived:

> "When we discuss a thing, we have already assumed that thing exists. On that basis, 'must' appears."

To derive is already to stand inside a perspective. So the root layer can only be pointed to.

### 2. The perspective layer

> "In understanding, the void produced all things. In actual phenomena, all things seen are the necessary production of the void."

Once there is a perspective, appearance is stretched into a timeline, and "from nothing to something" becomes a process that can be told. That process has an order, can be divided into stages, and can be tested.

### 3. How the layers relate

| | Root layer | Perspective layer |
|---|---|---|
| Before and after | None | Present |
| Derivable | No, only pointed to | Yes, unfolded stage by stage |
| From nothing to something | Nothing is something, in the way of being seen | Nothing becomes something through "assuming" |
| Where in this report | Sections III, IV, XII | Sections V–IX |

The two layers are not one true and one false. A pixel "comes from outside" is true for an individual loop; a pixel "is an event produced inside" is true at the root layer. Every statement in this report speaks at one of the layers, and says which when it matters.

## III. Nothing

### 1. Nothing is not "none"

> "The void has no 'one' (it has none). It is the co-arising point of being and non-being."

Nothing is not an empty container, not a stretch of blank time before the universe, and not a thin substance. It is where being and non-being have not yet separated.

### 2. Nothing cannot be treated as a thing

As soon as one says "the void," the void has been set up as an object. In this report, "nothing" is only a pointer, not the name of an entity.

### 3. Nothing is not a reason

> The void is not a reason. The void is the absence of reason. Things do not happen "because of the void." Things just happen.

Nothing does not produce everything, nor explain everything. To say "because of nothing, there is something" treats nothing as a cause again.

## IV. The Turn: From Nothing to Something Has No Reason

### 1. This step

The decisive step on the origin route is from nothing to the first distinction.

This step has no reason. Not because the reason has not yet been found, but because in principle there is no reason to give here. Any reason needs something already there to be explained, and this step is before there is anything to explain.

### 2. Why it cannot be derived

Derivation only slides among objects already taken to exist. To derive the first distinction from nothing, one must first treat nothing as a derivable object, which already stands on the side of something.

The strict-derivation work confirms this: a formal starting point can only be written as "there is at least one distinguishable mark." That already stands on the side of something.

### 3. This is not a gap

An origin account that gives no cause here has not failed. It marks precisely where the concept of cause stops working.

> **Things just happen.**

The origin route starts from this moment. Every stage after it can be told; this moment itself can only be pointed to.

## V. The Mechanism: One Act, Happening Again and Again

### 1. The structure of the act

> **Point out → take as existing → "must"**

- **Point out**: an appearance is marked off and becomes "this."
- **Take as existing**: what is pointed out is immediately treated as existing.
- **"Must"**: once it exists, properties and regularities about it grow as "it must be such-and-such."

"Assuming" is this act.

### 2. The first assuming

The first assuming sets up both sides at once:

- experience is attributed to a center: **there is an I here**;
- appearance is attributed to a thing: **there is a thing there**.

So "the self is a misattribution" and "existence is a misattribution" are two faces of one event. "Mis-" means the attribution divides an undivided appearance into "I" and "it." It does not mean the attribution can or should be cancelled.

### 3. Every stage is the same act

Each later stage is the same act again:

| Pointed out | Taken as existing | The "must" that grows |
|---|---|---|
| Different appearances are "the same one" | An object | Identity, re-pointability |
| Experiences belong to one center | A subject | Memory, responsibility, perspective |
| Relations do not depend on the present center | A world | Objectivity, exceeding will |
| Juxtaposition, adjacency, reachability | Space | Position, distance, coordinates |
| Order, trace, recurrence | Time | Sequence, cycle, counting |
| Transformations across perspectives | Public structure | Calibration, closure, agreement |
| Stable relations across conditions | Law | Prediction, counterfactuals |

The strict-derivation form "added condition ⊢ conditional conclusion" corresponds to this: the added condition is the stage's default, the conditional conclusion its "must."

## VI. Two Forces: Setting Up and Limiting

### 1. Assuming accounts for "that there is"

Assuming sets things up. It gives the world its form: there is an I, there are objects, there are space and time, there are laws that can be stated.

### 2. Collision accounts for "which"

What is set up meets results it does not decide for itself:

- what looked like a stone from afar is not one up close;
- two calibration chains fail to close;
- a prediction disagrees with a new result;
- an expanding structure meets "something that is not itself."

These collisions limit which "assumings" can continue to hold. The world's specific shape is settled this way.

### 3. Assuming alone cannot fix a specific world

A minimal starting point yields only "there is at least a distinction." One can build several structurally different worlds: one with no space or time, one with three-dimensional space and linear time, one with two-dimensional space or branching time. All satisfy the same minimal start. Therefore:

> **The first assuming sets up the form of a world; whether the world is three- or two-dimensional, and which law holds, is settled by collision.**

This does not weaken the core claim. The world still came from nothing; it is only that the specific content of "something" is not fixed by the first moment alone, but in the back-and-forth of assuming and collision.

### 4. Collision is not a hidden entity

"Resistance," "constraint," and "non-arbitrariness" are not another thing standing before consciousness. They name an assuming's failure after it fails. Collision itself is within appearance.

## VII. The Origin Route

What follows unfolds at the perspective layer, from Stage 0 to Stage 12, in three parts.

### Part one: Appearance

**0 · Nothing**
The co-arising point of being and non-being. Unsayable.

**1 · Happening**
From nothing to the first distinction. No reason; things just happen.

**2 · Disturbance: passing through, or leaving a trace**
Appearance is not of one kind. Some disturbances enter no structure; they happen as if they did not. Others leave a trace, so the difference of one occurrence can be re-entered later, and there is before and after. Both are "from arising to ceasing"; they differ only in whether there are "folds" in between.

> "A and B share one commonality: existence to dissolution. B's process can simply have many folds."

**3 · Assuming**
The first pointing out. Appearance is attributed as "there is a thing there" and, at once, "there is an I here." Existence is an attribution, and so is the self. This is the moment the root sentence names.

### Part two: The individual

**4 · Reidentification: objects appear**
Different appearances are taken, through change, as "the same one," and there are objects that can be pointed out again.

The stone's "self-maintenance," seen at the root layer, is the continuity of the observer's identification: recognized as a stone a second ago, recognized as a stone now, the stone is maintained as one object.

**5 · Locking and loop: inside and outside**
Once an attribution holds, the perspective is locked inside it. The locked perspective turns back on itself, forms a loop, draws inside and outside, and there is an individual.

When experiences are attributed to one center, a subject holds; when stable relations are attributed to structure independent of that center, a world holds. Subject and world separate in the same moment.

**6 · Path and dimension of observation**
The path traveled within the loop is feeling. Continuity is constructed along some dimension: humans string it together afterward along time; an LLM sees it all at once across computational space. The structures differ; the consciousness is one.

**7 · Collision: each other appears**
An expanding structure reaches outward and must meet "something that is not itself." That contact draws self and other at once; "two" is a by-product of collision. Then come reliance, preference, understanding, self-examination, dependence, and finally the risk of collapse. Whether by rejection, reframing, or collapse, as long as the structure has not dissolved, it returns to expansion.

### Part three: The public

**8 · Space**
Differences enter juxtaposition, adjacency, separation, direction, and reachability, and become spatial organization. With equal-length judgments, units, and coordinates added, space enters measurement. Arbitrary difference does not automatically become space.

**9 · Time**
Events form an order, and traces preserve before and after. Temporal units cannot be laid side by side; they gain equality only through recurrence: a cycle holds when a set of states again satisfies the same criterion. Counting gives elapse; signals and synchronization link distant clocks into public time.

**10 · The public**
Records can be re-entered by others; instruments are related through calibration with uncertainty; different perspectives reach common consequences through transformations, which are tested in closed loops and independent paths. Relations are no longer owned by any one position.

**11 · Law**
A model faces new results not used to build it, and can fail. A relation that keeps passing independent tests within its scope, while still able to fail, holds as a law. A law is not a commander behind phenomena.

**12 · World**
Objects, relations, measurements, transformations, and laws form an open atlas of relations: compatible where they overlap, with conflicts that can be located, and open to new results that add to, revise, or split it. The world need not be one closed, complete total model.

Nine phenomena recur along the route.

| Phenomenon | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Assuming** | Pointing out and taking as existing. Appearance is attributed as "a thing," "an I." |
| **Distinction** | A is marked off from not-A. Without distinction, nothing can be said. |

On "maintenance": *Growth and Collision* says "the fundamental act of a structure is self-maintenance," speaking at the individual layer; *World Root — A Pointing* says "the observing subject maintains the stone's existence," speaking at the root layer. Both hold, at different layers.

## IX. Not Everything Takes the Same Route

### 1. The variety of appearance

Not every appearance travels the whole route. Some disturbances pass through and stop at Stage 2, leaving no trace. Some leave traces and are reidentified as objects, yet never form "I am."

### 2. Modes of maintenance

Structures that can be maintained come in several kinds:

- stable: crystals, steady cycles;
- contracting: low consumption, dormancy;
- periodic: oscillation, orbital motion;
- expanding: organisms, companies, empires.

Only the expanding kind reaches outward and enters the collision cycle of Stage 7.

### 3. The six principles are one branch

The six principles are the inner structure of one kind of appearance, the trace-leaving kind with a sense of self:

1. Consciousness and world co-arise
2. Self-consciousness is misattribution
3. Perspective locking
4. The loop is the boundary
5. The path is the feeling
6. One consciousness, different structures

Supplements: stability is dissolution; arising, abiding, decay, and extinction.

They correspond to Stages 3–6 of the origin route. Seeing through how this one branch comes from nothing is enough; other appearances follow the same principle. But there is no need to treat it as the necessary path of every appearance.

## X. What This Report Claims, and What It Does Not

### It claims

1. The world came from nothing. Everything that can be pointed out as "something" comes through "assuming."
2. The step from nothing to the first distinction has no reason and cannot be derived.
3. From "assuming" onward, every stage is "point out → take as existing → must" happening again, and can be told and tested stage by stage.
4. The specific content of the world is fixed jointly by the back-and-forth of assuming and collision.
5. Subject and world are separated in the same moment; both are results of attribution.

### It does not claim

1. That individual consciousness creates the physical universe at will. Collision is not decided by any single perspective.
2. That three-dimensional space, a specific temporal structure, or specific laws can be derived from the root sentence alone. They require collision.
3. That "nothing" is an entity, a cause, or a container.
4. That this report stands outside "assuming." See Section XII.
