# The Basic Coordinates of All Things — A General System for Describing How Things Hold

> Zhou Peng & Codex
> 2026-07-30
> From difference, boundary, and trace to systems, publicity, law, and an open world

---

## Abstract

This study proposes a basic coordinate system for describing all things.

It is not a periodic table and does not say what material a thing is made of. Nor is it a parameter set that can generate real objects from nothing. It addresses another level:

> **Through what relations is anything distinguished, sustained, organized, changed, measured, publicly confirmed, and entered into a world?**

The system contains sixteen dimensions: difference, boundary, trace, reidentification, attribution, space, time, measurement, composition, interaction, feedback, selection and self-maintenance, emergence, publicity, law, and openness.

Anything that can be indicated as “some thing” can receive a structured projection onto these coordinates. A thing need not occupy every coordinate; not-applicable, unknown, and conflict are legitimate values. A stone, lightning strike, tree, pain, number, and state can enter one framework while forming radically different configurations.

The study does not claim that the sixteen coordinates exhaust every possible relation. Universality means open coverage: new things should enter existing coordinates, while an irreducible new relation must force a coordinate to be added or revised. Openness is therefore not an afterthought but the final condition of the system's validity.

---

## I. Theoretical Status

### Coordinates, not materials

Particles, fields, molecules, and cells answer what a concrete thing is made of. These coordinates answer how something holds as a distinguishable, persisting, acting, and measurable thing.

Difference is not a substance, boundary is not a smallest particle, and time is not an object manufactured by other coordinates. They are relational dimensions for describing modes of constitution.

### Meta-coordinates

Ordinary coordinates locate an object in an already given space. Meta-coordinates explain why an object can be indicated, how it keeps identity, how it enters spatial and temporal relations, how it forms systems, and how claims about it undergo public testing.

This is not another map inside the world. It describes how maps, objects, observers, and tests hold together.

### Description is not generation

The system may represent a tree, but coordinate values alone do not grow one.

> **Coordinates specify describable relational positions; occurrences and empirical data supply actual values.**

## II. Formal Definition

Let:

> `Q = {D,B,T,I,A,S,τ,M,C,R,F,V,E,P,L,O}`

be the sixteen-coordinate set. For thing `X` under context `K`—observation condition, scale, task, and time—define:

> `Φ(X | K) = <D,B,T,I,A,S,τ,M,C,R,F,V,E,P,L,O>X,K`

A coordinate need not be numerical. Its values include:

- `⊥`: not applicable;
- `?`: unknown;
- `!`: conflicting evidence;
- a relation, model, value, probability distribution, or evidence network.

### Non-orthogonality and non-minimality

“Coordinates” here are not independent axes in a linear space. Dimensions can be coupled: traces enter temporal judgment, interactions form feedback, composition supports emergence, and public tests revise laws.

The study does not prove that all sixteen dimensions are logically independent or form an irreducible minimal basis. “Basic” means that each carries a distinct diagnostic question. If coordinates are compressed, those questions must remain distinguishable inside the compressed structure.

`Φ` is therefore closer to a non-orthogonal atlas of local charts than to a vector space with coordinatewise linear addition.

A complete description is:

> `Desc(X | K) = <DataX,K, Φ(X | K), EvidenceX,K>`

`Data` supplies actual content and observations. `Φ` organizes relations of constitution. `Evidence` records provenance, uncertainty, and validation status.

Without data, coordinates are empty form. Without coordinates, data are unorganized records. Without evidence, one cannot distinguish judgment, hypothesis, and tested relation.

## III. Coordinate Group One: How a Thing Is Indicated

### 1. Difference `D`

What differs X from something else?

Difference supplies minimal discriminability. If X is indiscernible from everything, “X” has no operational content.

Failure: difference is announced only by a name and cannot be reproduced in any operation.

### 2. Boundary `B`

What counts as X and what does not?

A boundary may be a physical surface, functional closure, statistical threshold, event interval, formal criterion, institutional jurisdiction, or a fuzzy boundary with graded membership and uncertainty. Cell, storm, and national boundaries differ in kind while all perform inclusion and exclusion.

Failure: the boundary moves freely after results arrive so every counterexample can be excluded.

### 3. Trace `T`

How does X enter later relations?

A trace may be material change, record, memory, state variable, consequence, or symbolic preservation. It makes one difference re-enterable in a later structure.

Failure: an alleged past state has no accessible consequence but is still treated as verified history.

### 4. Reidentification `I`

Why is X still indicated as X after change?

Tracking may use material continuity, spatial path, causal history, functional organization, names and records, or formal isomorphism.

Failure: either every change preserves identity or every minute change creates a new object. Both destroy discrimination.

### 5. Attribution `A`

To whom or what are X's states, effects, experiences, or responsibilities attributed?

Attribution establishes positions of subject, object, cause, author, owner, and responsibility. It may be correct, mistaken, or disputed.

Failure: attribution is insulated from correction, or correlation is treated directly as agency.

---

## IV. Coordinate Group Two: How a Thing Is Situated

### 6. Space `S`

How is X juxtaposed, adjacent, separate, contained, oriented, and reachable relative to other things?

The coordinate may include topology, paths, regions, reference frames, and three-dimensional location. For nonphysical objects, `S` may be `⊥` or concern only symbolic realization; metaphorical space must not be passed off as physical position.

### 7. Time `τ`

When does X occur, persist, change, recur, or end?

Values may be event partial orders, intervals, cycles, historical stages, or local clock readings. A mathematical object need not occupy physical time, although inscriptions, proofs, and communication concerning it do.

### 8. Measurement `M`

Which relations of X can be compared, equated, counted, and assigned uncertainty?

Measurement includes units, comparison operations, values, scales, errors, and calibration chains.

Failure: a number without unit, procedure, scope, or uncertainty is treated as an absolute property carried by the thing.

---

## V. Coordinate Group Three: How a Thing Is Composed and Operates

### 9. Composition `C`

What are X's parts, and of what is X a part?

Composition records part–whole relations, levels, modules, interfaces, and scales. A whole is not automatically a list of parts; the organizing relation must be stated.

Failure: whole-properties are inferred directly from membership without explaining organization.

### 10. Interaction `R`

How does X change other things and undergo change?

> `R(X,Y,c) → <X',Y'>`

under condition `c`.

Interactions may be physical forces, information exchange, economic transactions, linguistic responses, or formal operations. Their categories may not be identified without translation.

### 11. Feedback `F`

How do the results of X's action re-enter the conditions of its later action?

> `state(t+1) = f(state(t), output(t), environment(t))`

Feedback may amplify, suppress, stabilize, or reorganize.

Failure: cyclic correlation is called control feedback without an identified return path.

### 12. Selection, Self-Maintenance, and Value `V`

Which states does the system retain, repair, approach, or avoid?

For an ordinary physical object, `V` may describe only stability conditions or be `⊥`. For organisms and agents it may include metabolism, goals, preferences, evaluation, and norms.

Value must not be projected onto everything without a selection mechanism, evaluative position, or functional criterion.

### 13. Emergence `E`

Which properties of the whole are absent from isolated parts yet supported by organization?

Emergence requires a cross-level bridge: microstates to macrovariables, stable macrorelations, and changes in macroproperties under changed organization.

Failure: temporary ignorance of a micro-explanation is merely renamed emergence.

---

## VI. Coordinate Group Four: How a Thing Becomes Public Knowledge

### 14. Publicity `P`

How do subjects, instruments, and expressions jointly refer to X?

Publicity includes records, reproducible operations, calibration, coordinate transformation, path closure, independent review, and dispute procedures.

Public does not mean identical feeling or truth by majority.

### 15. Law `L`

Which relations concerning X predict across conditions, and under what results do they fail?

At minimum:

> `L = <variables, conditions, relation, domain, uncertainty, failure criterion>`

A description that excludes no outcome lacks empirical-law content.

### 16. Openness `O`

How do new evidence, scales, and relations enter and revise the description of X?

Openness records unknowns, conflicts, revision history, version relations, and conditions for adding coordinates. It prevents current language from becoming the world's final boundary.

---

## VII. Five Basic Operations

### Projection

> `πq(Φ(X|K))`

reads one coordinate. Projection loses information and may not be substituted for the whole thing.

### Composition

> `Φ(X ⊕ Y | K)`

is not generally coordinatewise addition. Boundaries, interactions, feedback, and emergence may generate new values.

### Scale transformation

> `Σk→k'(Φ(X|K))`

maps molecular, cellular, individual, and ecological descriptions. It must state what is preserved, averaged, or lost.

### Perspective transformation

> `TK→K'(Φ(X|K))`

relates positions, units, languages, and instruments. Without transformation, two descriptions cannot be declared co-referential by name alone.

### Revision

> `Φt+1 = Revise(Φt, new evidence)`

may alter boundary, identity, composition, law, or scope. Reasons and versions must remain visible rather than silently erasing failures.

---

## VIII. Universal Description Theorem

### Proposition

> Any X that can be stably indicated as some thing in a context can be projected into `Q`, with not-applicable, unknown, and conflict admitted as coordinate values.

### Proof

Stable indication requires at least a difference between X and non-X, `D`, and a context-relative reference range, `B`. Each remaining coordinate can then receive a structured value, `⊥`, `?`, or `!`. Therefore `Φ(X|K)` can always be constructed. QED.

### Limit

This is a weak existence-of-representation result, close to question-coverage by definition—not explanatory adequacy or informational completeness. It does not prove that a projection losslessly reconstructs X, that different things receive unique projections, that every formally allowed tuple exists, or that sixteen coordinates never require extension.

> `Φ` is not guaranteed injective: `X ≠ Y` may still have `Φ(X|K)=Φ(Y|K)`.
>
> `Φ` is not guaranteed surjective onto reality: an arbitrary tuple need not correspond to a real thing.

The system is a general descriptive language, not a world generator.

## IX. Six Coordinate Projections

| Thing | Prominent coordinates | Weak/not applicable | Key validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| stone | `D B I S τ M C R L` | `V` and active feedback usually weak | tracking, material, measurement, collision |
| lightning | `D B T S τ R M L` | weak long-term identity | event boundary, multi-instrument record |
| tree | `B I τ C R F V E P L` | none categorically | metabolism, repair, growth, ecology |
| pain | `D B T I A τ M P O` | physical space partially applies | limited correspondence of report, action, physiology |
| number 2 | `D B I C P L O` | physical `S τ R` of the object are `⊥` | definition, proof, symbol transformation |
| political state | `B T I A C R F V E P L O` | no single material surface | law, record, institutional action, revision |

One coordinate can have different value-types. A stone boundary may be material, a state boundary legal-geographical, and a pain boundary experiential. Shared coordinates do not imply a shared entity type.

## X. Workflow for Describing a Thing

For candidate X:

1. Reference: how does X differ from non-X?
2. Boundary: what determines its range?
3. History: what traces support occurrence and continuity?
4. Identity: how is it reidentified through change?
5. Attribution: where are states, effects, and responsibility assigned?
6. Situation: what are its space, time, and scale?
7. Quantification: what is comparable and with what uncertainty?
8. System: how do parts, interactions, feedback, selection, and emergence organize?
9. Publicity: how can others and instruments re-enter it?
10. Law: what does it predict, and what would defeat it?
11. Openness: how are unknowns, conflicts, and revisions retained?

Every conclusion should carry a definition, data source, operation, evidence grade, context, failure condition, and version.

---

## XI. Completeness of the Coordinate System

“Describing everything” has at least three strengths.

### Coverage completeness

Every referable thing receives a projection. This study claims it while `⊥ ? !` remain valid.

### Discriminative completeness

Every different thing receives a unique projection. This study does not claim it; values may be coarse and contexts may hide differences.

### Generative completeness

Coordinate values alone produce every actual thing. This study rejects it. Actual content, initial conditions, and empirical constraint do not come from empty coordinates.

> **The system seeks open coverage completeness, not closed discriminative or generative completeness.**

---

## XII. Falsification and Extension

The coordinate set fails coverage if a candidate X:

1. can be stably referred to;
2. has an irreducible condition of holding that no coordinate expresses even with structured values, `⊥`, `?`, and `!`;
3. cannot reduce that condition to existing coordinates or operations;
4. exhibits the same need across cases.

A new coordinate must explain a previously inexpressible difference, have an operational or strict criterion, avoid duplication, show what is lost when removed, and state where it applies.

Openness therefore does not evade failure. It requires failure to have structural consequences.

---

## XIII. Core Formulae

> **Thing = a difference bounded and trackable.**

> **System = a constraint network formed by composition, interaction, and feedback among things.**

> **Public object = a compatibility class of local descriptions formed through records, transformation, calibration, and independent testing.**

> **Law = a public relation producing fallible predictions within a stated domain.**

> **World = an atlas of local networks in which objects, events, systems, measurements, and laws can translate, constrain one another, and remain open to new relations.**

Unified:

> `Desc(X | K) = <Data, ΦQ, Evidence>`

Reality supplies content, coordinates organize how it holds as a thing, and evidence constrains why the description holds.

---

## XIV. Final Conclusion

What we now possess is not a final theory of everything, but a more basic tool:

> **a general meta-coordinate system for describing how anything holds as a thing.**

It can ask common questions of stones, events, organisms, experiences, mathematical objects, institutions, and laws without erasing their categorical differences.

Its universality comes from four features:

1. every reference requires difference and boundary;
2. all other coordinates allow structures, not-applicable, unknown, and conflict;
3. complex things enter through composition, scale, transformation, and revision;
4. an inexpressible new thing can force the coordinate set to expand.

The precise thesis is:

> **Anything that can be indicated as some thing can be projected onto these basic coordinates; the projection describes how it holds, but is not the thing itself and does not exhaust every possible appearance in advance.**

Therefore:

> **We have a basic coordinate system for describing the constitutive structure of all things.**
>
> **The concrete world is the totality of actual configurations these coordinates take under real constraint, together with new occurrences that continue to force their revision.**
