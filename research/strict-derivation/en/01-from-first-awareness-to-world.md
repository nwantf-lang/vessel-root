> From minimal discriminable appearance to objects, spacetime, public measurement, laws, and a world-whole

---

## Abstract

This study tests a strong claim: can the whole world be rigorously derived from “first awareness”?

The answer has two parts.

First, if derivation means obtaining three-dimensional space, continuous time, matter, other subjects, and concrete physical laws by logic from one minimal awareness premise, the answer is no. A countermodel proves this: mutually different world-structures satisfy the same minimal premise, so no concrete world is its logical consequence.

Second, a rigorous conditional construction is possible when no added premise is hidden. Minimal discriminable appearance, supplemented step by step with trace, reidentification, attribution, spatial and temporal organization, measurement, public calibration, empirical constraint, and lawlike modeling, yields a world as a public and revisable relational whole.

Rigor here means:

1. every primitive is defined;
2. every added condition is numbered;
3. every conclusion carries a proof type;
4. every step states a counterexample or failure condition;
5. constitutive conclusions are not passed off as physical causes;
6. empirical premises are submitted to observation and experiment rather than “proved” by philosophy.

## I. What Counts as a Rigorous Derivation

Let `P0` be the minimal starting point, `A1 … An` added conditions, and `Ck` a conclusion at level `k`. A valid derivation has the form:

> `P0, A1, …, Ak ⊢ Ck`

If a model satisfies every premise on the left but not `Ck`, then:

> `P0, A1, …, Ak ⊬ Ck`

The conditions must then be strengthened, the conclusion narrowed, or the derivation rejected.

### Four proof labels

| Label | Meaning | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| `[D]` | true by definition | verify that a stipulation is not presented as a discovery |
| `[L]` | logically necessary | test every model satisfying the premises |
| `[C]` | conditional theorem | expose added conditions and verify the inference |
| `[E]` | empirically pending | observation, instruments, replication, uncertainty, and failure |

Philosophy can establish `[D]`, `[L]`, and some `[C]`. Any concrete claim about how reality actually behaves carries `[E]`.

We also distinguish:

- `Wc`: world as an experienceable, referable, publicly testable relational whole;
- `Wp`: the complete concrete content of the physical universe.

This study conditionally constructs `Wc`. It does not derive `Wp` from minimal awareness.

### Sufficiency is not unique necessity

The claim proved is:

> `P0 + A1 + … + A10` is one explicit sufficient route for constituting `Wc`.

The study does not prove that `A1 … A10` is the only possible route or that every item is metaphysically necessary for every possible world. A failure test shows that removing an item no longer guarantees the corresponding result **as defined in this construction**. It does not exhaust all possible constructions.

## II. Minimizing “First Awareness”

“First” cannot mean the earliest cosmic instant, because that would presuppose time. “Awareness” cannot mean a property owned by a subject, because that would presuppose a subject.

Its minimal form is:

> `P0`: there is at least one discriminable mark.

Let `E` be a nonempty field of appearance and `D` a discrimination relation, with `D(x,y) → x ≠ y`:

> `∃a∃b D(a,b)`

`b` may be only an unmarked “not-a.” `D(a,b)` says that the two are not wholly indiscernible.

This is not an empirical account of brains, minds, or cosmic origins. It is the minimal constitutive definition: without any difference, nothing can be called a first awareness `[D]`.

### Conclusion 0

> `C0`: minimal awareness contains at least discriminability.

This unfolds `P0`; it is `[D]`, not a causal explanation from an earlier entity.

Test: remove `D`. “There is awareness” and “there is none” can no longer differ expressibly, so the starting point loses content.

It does not yield a subject, object, order, space, matter, or law.

## III. Impossibility Theorem: Minimal Awareness Does Not Determine a Concrete World

### Theorem 1: underdetermination

> `P0` alone entails no particular spatial dimension, temporal structure, or physical law.

### Proof

Construct:

- `M1`, with `a,b` and `D(a,b)`, but no space, time, or dynamics;
- `M2`, with the same minimal facts plus three-dimensional Euclidean coordinates, linear time, and one dynamics;
- `M3`, also satisfying `P0`, but with two-dimensional space, branching time, or a different dynamics.

Their reducts to the minimal language all satisfy `P0`, while they disagree over three-dimensionality, linear time, and particular laws. Therefore:

> `P0 ⊬ three-dimensional space`
>
> `P0 ⊬ linear time`
>
> `P0 ⊬ any particular physical law`

QED `[L]`.

Any claimed direct derivation must hide premises, treat definitions as discoveries, confuse conceivability with physical fact, or import known features of our world into the start.

The derivation may continue only by **explicit extension**: every level obtains a conditional conclusion under an openly added condition.

## IV. Level One: From Difference to Trace and Order

### Added condition `A1`

There are accessible items `e0,e1,…`, an asymmetric relation `≺`, and a trace function `τ`. If `ei ≺ ej`, then not `ej ≺ ei`, and some difference of `ei` can be re-entered at `ej`.

### Conclusion 1

> `C1`: experiential order and the availability of a prior difference can be defined.

Proof: asymmetry orients the relation; `τ` relates items across it `[C]`.

Tests:

- if both `ei ≺ ej` and `ej ≺ ei`, strict precedence fails;
- if `τ` is always empty, earlier items cannot enter judgment, count, or history.

It does not yield physical time, temporal continuity, or a universal arrow. `A1` adds order; order was not created from difference alone.

---

## V. Level Two: From Trace to Reidentification and Object

### Added condition `A2`

At each evidence state `t`, add a revisable tracking relation `Rt` that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive within that fixed state:

> `[x]Rt = {y ∈ E | Rt(x,y)}`

defines an object-class.

### Conclusion 2

> `C2`: different appearances can be organized into reidentifiable objects.

Proof: at fixed `t`, an equivalence relation partitions its domain and each class supplies identity across appearances. New evidence may replace `Rt` with `Rt+1`, revising the partition `[C]`.

Tests include identity through viewpoint and property change, evidence-driven splitting or merging, and failure of predictions based on identity.

It does not yield an object existing independently of all experience. It yields objecthood and reidentifiability, not final ontology.

---

## VI. Level Three: From Object Relations to Subject–World Differentiation

### Added condition `A3`

Add:

- `S(e)`, attributing experience, memory, and action to an experiential center;
- `O(r)`, attributing stable relations to object structures independent of the present center.

Require that changing present intention need not change the outcomes expected under `O(r)`.

### Conclusion 3

> `C3a`: unified attribution of experiences constitutes a subject-position.
>
> `C3b`: attribution of relations beyond the present center constitutes a world-direction.

The maps define two attribution classes; intention/outcome separation prevents their collapse into one source of control `[D]+[C]`.

Tests: unity of memory, report, and action; predictions beyond present will; correction through illusion, memory error, and failed action.

This does not yield an eternal self, a wholly external world, or substance dualism. It yields functional positions.

---

## VII. Level Four: From Objects to Spatial Organization

### Added condition `A4`

Add adjacency `Adj`, separation `Sep`, containment `In`, direction `Dir`, paths, and reachability `Reach` to object-classes. Adjacency is at least symmetric; finite paths connect adjacent nodes; reachability is their path closure.

### Conclusion 4

> `C4`: object relations form a spatial organization.

Objects become graph nodes, adjacency edges, paths connected components, while containment and direction add regions and orientation `[C]`.

Tests: repeatable paths, preservation of endpoints across routes, consistency of containment/separation/direction, and rule-governed updates under operation.

This does not yield distance, numerical angle, dimensionality, Euclidean geometry, or physical space. Arbitrary difference does not yield `A4`.

---

## VIII. Level Five: From Order to Temporal Organization

### Added condition `A5`

Extend `A1` to a strict event partial order: irreflexive and transitive, while allowing incomparable events. Define `Ch(x,i,j)` when one tracked object has different states at ordered positions.

### Conclusion 5

> `C5`: events form temporal relations of precedence, ordered event-pairs, and change.

The partial order supplies precedence. If `e1 ≺ e2`, `<e1,e2>` supplies an as-yet unmeasured event interval. `Rt` with `Ch` supplies state-change of one object; traces make the order later recognizable `[C]`.

Tests: absence of order cycles, trace consistency, preservation of genuine incomparability, and distinction between change and object replacement.

This does not yield continuous time, a universal present, physical irreversibility, or a time scale.

---

## IX. Level Six: From Organization to Measurable Relations

### Added condition `A6`

For a class of intervals add:

- substitutability `≈` under stated operations;
- composition `⊕`;
- a unit-class `U`;
- counting `nU = U ⊕ … ⊕ U`;
- uncertainty `ε` and calibration.

Spatial intervals use allowed transport and coincidence; temporal intervals use recurring cycles and accumulated traces.

### Conclusion 6

> `C6`: intervals finitely covered by a unit obtain integer or rational measurement expressions.

If `I ≈ nU`, define `μ(I)=n`. If a unit is validly divided into `m` parts, define `μ(I)=n/m`. Composition preserves `μ(I⊕J)=μ(I)+μ(J)` `[C]`.

Tests: unit replication and transport, path comparison, calibration closure, uncertainty propagation, and stability under changed environments.

This does not yield the real continuum, a unique metric, an absolute ruler or clock, or a concrete unit. Real-valued continuous representation needs stronger completeness assumptions.

---

## X. Level Seven: From Local Measurement to Public Objectivity

### Added condition `A7`

There are local systems `Li`, each with records, instruments, and uncertainty, and composable transformations:

> `Tji : Li → Lj`

Require identity, local invertibility within uncertainty, composition, path closure, and at least partly independent measurement routes.

### Conclusion 7

> `C7`: local readings form public compatibility classes owned by no single perspective.

In the exact, zero-uncertainty case, identity, composition, and inversion give a groupoid-like structure whose orbits are strict equivalence classes. With actual uncertainty, only compatibility classes under an explicit error budget and path length follow; if accumulated error defeats transitivity, they must not be called equivalence relations `[C]`.

Tests: `A→B→A` closure; agreement of `A→B→C` with `A→C`; cross-checks by instruments using different principles; holdout data.

This does not yield truth by consensus, elimination of all error, disappearance of perspective, or final reality.

---

## XI. Level Eight: From Public Agreement to Non-Arbitrary Constraint

### Added condition `A8`

After construction, a model must face a new result `y` not wholly fixed by its training data. It gives an allowed set `Pred(M,c)`. If:

> `y ∉ Pred(M,c)`

failure is recorded. Model, condition, and criterion may not all be changed costlessly after the fact.

### Conclusion 8

> `C8`: public structure gains empirical content rather than internal coherence alone.

The model excludes at least one result; a new result can defeat it, so it bears information risk `[C]`. What reality supplies as `y` remains `[E]`.

Tests: preregistered criteria, holdout samples, new places/times/instruments, intervention, and consequential revision after failure.

This does not turn constraint into a mysterious extra-conscious substance. Constraint names non-arbitrariness after prediction and result are compared.

---

## XII. Level Nine: From Constraint to Law

### Added condition `A9`

A candidate law has:

> `L = <V, C, F, Ω, ε>`

where `V` are measurable variables, `C` conditions or interventions, `F` a relation or probability rule, `Ω` its domain, and `ε` its uncertainty or statistical criterion. It must predict conditions not used in construction.

### Conclusion 9

> `C9`: a relation that continues to pass independent tests within `Ω` while retaining a possibility of failure can hold as a physical law.

The definition supplies its expression; cross-condition testing supplies provisional warrant. The former is `[D]+[C]`; actual success is `[E]`.

Tests: predictive calibration, counterfactuals and interventions, cross-condition stability, comparison with rival models, and recognition of out-of-domain failure.

This does not logically guarantee an infinite future from finite success, nor make law a commander behind phenomena.

---

## XIII. Level Ten: From a Network of Laws to a World-Whole

### Added condition `A10`

Organize validated objects, relations, measurements, transformations, and models as a family of local networks:

> `N = <O, R, M, T, L>`

Require compatibility on overlaps, localization of conflict, revision by new results, and no presumption of closure or completeness.

Not every network must be forced into one contradiction-free total model. Define:

> `Wc` as an open atlas consisting of valid local networks, translations among them, known conflicts, and rules of revision.

### Conclusion 10

> `C10`: a world holds as a public, structured, and revisable open relational atlas.

Compatible networks can be jointly used through translations; incompatible ones retain conflict edges rather than being forcibly merged; new structures can add nodes, revise translations, or rearrange conflicts. A public whole is obtained without assuming one unique maximal union `[D]+[C]`.

Tests: cross-domain coreference, translation of units/coordinates/models, executable tests generated by conflict, revision under novelty, and detection of evidence excluded from entry.

This does not show that the network contains every fact, that `Wc = Wp`, or that the world has one final description.

## XV. Validation Status

“Validation” is limited as follows:

- `C0` and the underdetermination theorem have been formally checked by definitional expansion and countermodel;
- `C1–C7` and `C10` have been checked for conditional validity, with failure modes supplied for removal of key premises;
- the formal parts of `C8–C9` are checked, but whether reality supplies matching outcomes cannot be established inside a text;
- every `[E]` item currently has a test protocol, not a status of “empirically passed.”

The report therefore proves that the inferential architecture is valid and its boundaries auditable. It does not claim that philosophical writing has verified every empirical premise. Without data, instruments, uncertainty, and independent replication, every `[E]` conclusion remains pending.
